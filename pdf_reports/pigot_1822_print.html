Pigot & Co.’s London & Provincial New Commercial Directory for 1822-23

Borough, Port & Ancient Market Town of Newport (with Caerleon)

Historical Topographical Survey & Commercial Statistics (1822)

A CORPORATION town of Monmouthshire, delightfully seated on the river Usk, over which is a handsome stone bridge, with five arches. It has a good harbour, whence it has its name; and is 148 miles from London, 28 from Bristol, 15 from the New Passage, 12 from Caerphilly, and 12 from Cardiff. It carries on an extensive trade in coals and iron, 300,000 tons of the former, and 42,000 tons of the latter, being annually exported, and is a flourishing town, as will be seen from the shipping account, and a comparative view of the census. The town is governed by a mayor, in conjunction with another magistrate; the mayor and common council (say 12) form the corporation. There is a church, called St. Woolo’s, situated on the outskirt of the town. In the town are several places of worship for dissenters; also a masonic hall, a neat, respectable edifice, with a superior freestone front, erected in 1821, and cost nearly 500l.; and a charity school, designated the British School, in which a great number of boys are taught the principles of education, supported with a strong inculcation of moral duties. It also contains a work-house; and in 1818, an excellent market-house was erected, which cost about 3000l. Market day is Saturday. The fairs held here are—Ascension Thursday, Nov. 6th, Aug. 15th, third Monday in the month a great market; at Stow, first Thursday after Whitsuntide, and another fair is established, but the day on which it is to be held is not fixed. In 1801, it contained 1,087 inhabitants; and in 1821, males 1,730, females 1,766—total 3,496; being an increase in 20 years of 2,409. Newport and Usk are represented in parliament by the Marquis of Worcester. Post-Office.—George Griffiths, Post Master.—Mail from London and all parts of the North of England, arrives at half-past three in the afternoon, and is despatched at nine in the morning.—Mail from South Wales arrives at half-past nine in the morning, and is despatched at three in the afternoon.
